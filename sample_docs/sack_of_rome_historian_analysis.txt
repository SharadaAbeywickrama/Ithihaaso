Modern Historiography on the Sack of Rome (Dr. E. Thompson, 2018)

The 410 AD Sack of Rome by Alaric is often remembered in popular history as a cataclysmic event of total devastation, largely due to the writings of elite Roman contemporaries who exaggerated the physical destruction to reflect their psychological trauma. 

In reality, archaeological evidence suggests the physical damage to the city was remarkably minimal. The Visigoths were primarily motivated by political and economic goals—specifically, securing agricultural land in the empire and payment for their military service. The Sack was a controlled, calculated political maneuver rather than a display of mindless barbarian destruction. It was less the 'fall' of civilization and more a forceful renegotiation of Gothic status within the Roman system.
